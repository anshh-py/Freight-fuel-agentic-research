## 1. Dataset and evaluation overview

This study is a **pipeline-validation on synthetic data**. The modelling dataset is a formula-generated suite of 963,697 rows of 1 Hz telemetry from 29 synthetic truck identities (classes 32T/40T/49T), generated by `scripts/synthetic_heavy_truck/gen_telemetry.py` (seed 20240617); the target `fuel_rate_lph` is **formula-generated, not measured** (`docs/M7_METHODOLOGY.md` §2; `data/synthetic_heavy_truck_research/dataset_card.json`). No real truck, live OBD, or validated fuel measurement exists in this work (`docs/REPRODUCIBILITY_RESEARCH.md` §1, §12). The FEAD real-archive provisioning is documented in `DATA_SOURCES.md`, but **the modelling dataset is the synthetic suite, not the FEAD archive** (`docs/M7_METHODOLOGY.md` §2.3).

The evaluation protocol is a **truck-wise holdout**: 20 train / 4 validation / 5 held-out test truck identities (rows 657,336 / 133,072 / 173,289; the test split has 96 trips). Models are trained on the 20 train trucks, selected on the 4 validation trucks by validation MAE, and evaluated once on the 5 held-out test trucks (`reports/m1/metrics_report.json`; `reports/m1/test_metrics_report.json`). The test set is used exactly once and never informs fitting, tuning, or selection. All model-level results below are **pooled over the 173,289 test rows** unless a per-truck or per-condition partition is explicitly stated; row-level aggregates are not treated as independent (rows within a truck/trip are autocorrelated) — per-truck and trip-level resampling is used (`docs/M7_METHODOLOGY.md` §5, §6.7).

## 8. Robustness and ablation results

### 8.1 Input-perturbation robustness (M3.4)

Synthetic corruption was applied to copies of the final model-input feature matrices (after feature construction, before preprocessing) on the frozen 5-truck test split — corruption **of synthetic copies**, not real-world sensor-failure validation. Prediction-failure rate is 0 in all scenarios; metrics are on surviving finite rows. Source: `reports/m3/telemetry_robustness_summary.md`.

**Table 8.1 — Selected perturbation scenarios, full-operational (baseline MAE 2.5684).** Source: `reports/m3/telemetry_robustness_summary.md`.

| Scenario                              | MAE (L/h) |      R² |     ΔMAE | ratio (vs clean) |
| ------------------------------------- | --------: | ------: | -------: | ---------------: |
| Random missingness 5%                 |    5.7634 |  0.8757 |  +3.1949 |             2.24 |
| Random missingness 30%                |   25.2789 | −0.1196 | +22.7105 |             9.84 |
| Complete sensor failure — engine_rpm  |    5.5840 |  0.9639 |  +3.0156 |             2.17 |
| Complete sensor failure — speed_kph   |    2.7228 |  0.9867 |  +0.1543 |             1.06 |
| Gaussian noise 25%                    |    6.5705 |  0.9465 |  +4.0021 |             2.56 |
| Gaussian noise 50%                    |   11.8161 |  0.8329 |  +9.2477 |             4.60 |
| Fixed bias — engine_rpm +200          |    4.0146 |  0.9768 |  +1.4462 |             1.56 |
| Clipping physical bounds              |    2.5684 |  0.9878 |   0.0000 |             1.00 |
| Combined missingness 20% + noise 0.25 |   19.3110 |  0.3356 | +16.7426 |             7.52 |

The model is **most sensitive to random missingness** (MAE ratio up to ~9.8× at 30% missingness; telematics-only reaches ~15.4×) and to complete failure of the engine-speed signal; it is robust to clipping and to small fixed biases in temperature/speed. These results characterize sensitivity to injected corruptions only.

### 8.2 Agent ablation (M5.5)

M5.5 compares ablated variants of the deterministic driver pipeline against the baseline `A_full_pipeline` (which equals M5.4, overall 0.920) on the frozen M5.4 rubric. **M5.5 is an offline engineering ablation; it is not formally closed as validation and is not human-subject or real-world validation** (`reports/m5/m5_5/summary.md`; `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY_RESEARCH.md` §12). Score differences are **descriptive absolute percentage-point deltas**; no inferential significance testing was performed.

**Table 8.2 — M5.5 ablation variants.** Source: `reports/m5/m5_5/summary.md`.

| Variant                       |                    Overall | Applicable | Passed | Δpp vs A | Safety-critical |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------: | ---------: | -----: | -------: | --------------: |
| A_full_pipeline (baseline)    |                      0.920 |        201 |    185 |        — |               0 |
| B_without_session_context     |                      0.908 |        196 |    178 |    −1.22 |               0 |
| C_without_retrieval_grounding |                      0.910 |        201 |    183 |    −1.00 |               0 |
| D_without_shap_explanation    |                      0.920 |        201 |    185 |     0.00 |               0 |
| E_without_response_validation |                shadow only |          — |      — |        — |               — |
| F_feature_set_comparison      | 0.920 (full) / 0.915 (tel) |          — |      — |        — |               0 |

Removing session context (B, −1.22pp) or retrieval grounding (C, −1.00pp) lowers the overall score slightly; removing SHAP explanation (D) leaves it unchanged at 0.920. Variant E is an **offline shadow** (13 turns evaluated; 5 turns were marked deliverable in the shadow; 0 validator failures in shadow; unvalidated text never delivered). These shadow outcomes are simulated deliverability decisions — **no external delivery occurred**, and no message was sent to any user or external system. Variant F compares feature sets only on compatible inputs (reusing M5.3 facts); **no model-level accuracy is recomputed and no superiority is claimed** from incomparable input sets. All safety-critical scenario counts are 0. These small deltas are descriptive; they are not evidence that any single component is or is not necessary.

Because variant B removes session context, its **applicable set differs (196 vs. 201 for A/C/D)**; B's Δpp is therefore computed against a different applicable population and is **not a strict same-denominator comparison** with A/C/D.

## 9. System integration results

## 10. Discussion

The four research questions (RQ1–RQ4, refined in `docs/M7_RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md` §7) are addressed below strictly within what the evidence supports. The overall position is a **synthetic pipeline-validation**: none of these results establish real-world predictive validity.

### RQ1 — How accurately can ML models predict fuel rate on the five held-out synthetic truck identities?

**What the evidence supports.** On the five held-out synthetic trucks, the validation-selected XGBoost model achieves pooled MAE 2.5684 L/h, RMSE 4.5275 L/h, R² 0.9878 (full-operational), far better than the mean-dummy floor (MAE 37.19). The trip-clustered bootstrap gives MAE 95% CI [2.222, 2.918]. Per-truck MAE ranges 1.30–3.87 L/h across the five trucks; error concentrates in accelerating/highway/steep-uphill conditions (§4). The truck-wise holdout protocol ensures every test truck was entirely unseen during training (§1).

**What the evidence does not support.** Generalization to any truck population, to real vehicles, or to heavier payload regimes. Only five test trucks exist; truck-level estimates are descriptive and unstable; the between-truck variance component is not a defensible population estimate (ANOVA/ICC withheld). High R² on **synthetic, formula-generated** targets is not evidence of real-world accuracy.

**Significance.** The protocol demonstrates a reproducible, leakage-free truck-wise evaluation pipeline — the methodological objective (Objective 1) — and quantifies uncertainty conditional on the five test trucks. This is a pipeline result, not a fleet-performance claim.

### RQ2 — What is the predictive-performance difference between the full-operational and telematics-only feature sets?

**What the evidence supports.** On these five held-out trucks, the two sets produce nearly identical pooled MAE (2.5684 vs. 2.5660; delta −0.0025 L/h). The trip-clustered bootstrap 95% CI for the delta is **[−0.237, +0.223]**, which straddles zero; per-truck deltas vary in sign. In condition-level paired comparisons the deltas are small and descriptive (e.g. full performs better on braking/idle; telematics better on accelerating/uphill; `reports/m3/error_diagnosis_summary.md` Experiment 5).

**What the evidence does not support.** Statistical equivalence, superiority of either set, or operational interchangeability. The CI straddling zero means the available evidence does **not** show a meaningful predictive difference — it also does **not** prove the sets are equivalent. Per-truck deltas are not significance-tested (n=5).

**Significance.** The feature comparison is informative in combination with §5: the two sets give similar scores but materially different SHAP explanations. Removing engine-internal signals changes _what the model attributes_ even when it does not change the aggregate score. This is a descriptive finding about the synthetic pipeline, not a recommendation for either feature set in practice.

### RQ3 — How effectively can SHAP provide faithful instance-level explanations of fuel-rate predictions?

**What the evidence supports.** SHAP reconstructions pass for both feature sets: max |deviation| 1.57e-4 (full) and 1.44e-4 (telematics), fraction within tolerance 1.0 — i.e. the SHAP decomposition reproduces the model output. Feature importance is dominated by engine-internal signals (torque, engine load, engine speed) in the full set and by motion/dynamics signals (acceleration, speed, grade) in the telematics set. This operationalizes "effective" as **fidelity to the model** on unseen trucks.

**What the evidence does not support.** Causality, human-judged interpretability, or real-world fuel drivers. SHAP is attribution for the synthetic-trained model; reconstruction validates additive consistency, not human comprehension.

**Significance.** Explaining predictions for _unseen_ trucks (the deployed scenario) with a reconstruction check is the distinct contribution here versus much of the literature, which explains in-sample or same-fleet models. The explanations are intended to be faithful to the model, and the agent is constrained to present them as attribution, not causation.

### RQ4 — How reliably can a retrieval-grounded driver-feedback agent interpret prediction and vehicle context while avoiding unsupported advice?

**What the evidence supports.** Within a **manually authored, deterministic benchmark**, the agent's intent classification, tool execution, prediction integrity, explanation integrity, and knowledge grounding all pass 100% (M2.4: 38/38 positive; 11/11 negative controls detected). The M5.4 rubric scores 0.920 overall with perfect safety and evidence/uncertainty sub-scores (46/46, 52/52) and zero safety-critical violations. On unanswerable retrieval cases the agent abstains (7/7) rather than fabricating; in the live browser it declined the unsupported query "How can I reduce fuel consumption?" and did not deliver ungrounded advice. This supports the guardrail/abstention design (H5).

**What the evidence does not support.** Real-world reliability, human-driver comprehension, statistical significance, or breadth. The benchmarks are hand-authored, the deterministic rubric is not a human-subject study, and M5.5 (ablation) is not formally closed as validation. Relevance (0.577) is a recognized weak dimension — rubric non-compliance concentrates there.

**Significance.** The evaluation is explicitly built around the failure modes the RAG literature highlights — especially **negative rejection** (Chen et al., 2024) — and the deterministic agent is scoped as an engineering artifact, not an LLM.

### Comparison with the 2026 conversational eco-driving study (Karlsson et al., 2026)

The closest related work combines prediction + SHAP + RAG + conversational coaching on a **real Volvo fleet** with per-segment LightGBM models and a five-expert evaluation. This project is **not** the first to combine these technologies, and we make no such claim. The distinction is one of scope and evidence class:

| Aspect         | Karlsson et al. (2026)                                                 | Present study                                                             |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Data           | Real vehicle-level telemetry (weekly–monthly aggregated), two segments | Synthetic, formula-generated per-second telemetry, 29 identities (20/4/5) |
| Prediction     | Fuel efficiency over aggregated windows, per segment                   | Instantaneous fuel rate (L/h), per row                                    |
| Model/eval     | LightGBM, cross-validation within segments (R² 0.796/0.744)            | XGBoost, truck-wise holdout (R² 0.988 on synthetic test)                  |
| Agent          | LLM + RAG + memory, conversational; expert evaluation                  | Deterministic rule-based agent; manually authored rubric                  |
| Generalization | Not leave-trucks-out                                                   | Truck-wise holdout, but synthetic only                                    |

The 2026 study does not evaluate cross-truck generalization and its expert panel found retrieval grounding did not consistently improve feedback; our contribution is a **synthetic cross-truck generalization + reconstruction-validated SHAP + abstention-oriented agent pipeline validation** — complementary, not competitive, and explicitly not real-world validation (`docs/M7_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md` §9).

## 11. Limitations and threats to validity

1. **Synthetic data.** All telemetry and targets are formula-generated; there is no real truck, live OBD, fleet, or validated fuel measurement. High R² on synthetic data is not evidence of real-world generalization; the target is generated, not measured.
2. **Five-truck test set.** Per-truck estimates, macro-averages, variance decomposition, and condition subgroups are descriptive and unstable; n=5 is too small for a defensible between-truck random-effect estimate (ANOVA/ICC withheld).
3. **Truck/route confound.** Truck and route are confounded in the reference setting; a truck-wise split may partly measure route shift.
4. **Single 49T truck.** The 49T class appears as one held-out truck; no class-level generalization is claimed.
5. **Empty heavy-payload regime.** The heavy-payload cell (load factor > 0.66) is empty by construction across the whole dataset; combined heavy cells cannot be evaluated. **No heavy-payload performance is claimed.**
6. **Autocorrelation.** Rows within a truck/trip are autocorrelated; per-truck and trip-level resampling is used, and row-level aggregates are not treated as independent.
7. **Manually authored agent benchmarks.** M2.4, M5.1–M5.5 are hand-authored, deterministic rubrics; they are not independent human-subject studies and claim no statistical significance or real-world reliability. The M5.4 relevance sub-score (0.577) is a limitation of rubric coverage.
8. **Deterministic agent.** The agent is a keyword/rule router, not an LLM; it offers no free-form natural-language understanding.
9. **M5.5 not formally closed; M6.5 video incomplete.** M5.5 is an offline engineering ablation (not closed as validation); the M6.5 demo video is unrecorded. Integration negative paths are largely covered by mocked component/API tests, not live-browser checks.
10. **Feature-set comparison on compatible inputs only** (M5.5 variant F) — no model-level accuracy recomputation and no superiority claim.
11. **No commercial validation.** Nothing here validates the system in commercial operations.

## 12. Research implications and future work

- **Methodological.** The truck-wise holdout + trip-clustered bootstrap protocol is a reproducible template for evaluating cross-truck transfer on synthetic telemetry; it could be carried to real fleet data.
- **Feature/explanation.** The finding that score-similar feature sets can have very different SHAP explanations motivates reporting score _and_ explanation together, and constrains how "feature-set parity" should be interpreted.
- **Agent design.** The abstention/guardrail pattern (including detection of the 11 negative controls and 7/7 unanswerable abstentions) is a transferable safety template for grounded driver-feedback assistants — evaluated here on a deterministic system, not an LLM.
- **Future work (not claimed here):** real fleet data and validated fuel measurement; more test trucks and a defensible between-truck random-effect analysis; heavy-payload regimes; human-subject evaluation of driver feedback; LLM-based agents with the same guardrail battery; completing M5.5 closure and the M6.5 video package.
